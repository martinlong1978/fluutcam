# Five-Axis Plan (3+2, then simultaneous, learning from CamCraft)

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Take FluutCAM from 3-axis, rotary (A) and indexed (A) setups to 3+2 with two angles, then to simultaneous 5-axis finishing with a ball tool. Take what CamCraft has already worked out, and do it on FluutCAM's exact B-rep surfaces instead of meshes.

**Status:** a plan for the owner to approve. The spec still says "Simultaneous 5-axis is future work" (OQ-16, S 03 §3.9.2, S 04 §4.7). Task 1 asks for that decision; nothing past Task 2 starts without it.

## What CamCraft has (surveyed 2026-11-14)

CamCraft ([NeoTech/Camcraft](https://github.com/NeoTech/Camcraft), Apache-2.0, about three weeks old, pre-1.0) lists 3+2 indexing, rotary contour, parallel and offset, 5-axis contour and swarf. Read from its source and guide:

| Piece | Where | What it does | Use to FluutCAM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tool-axis law | `crates/motion/src/multiaxis/tilt.rs` | At a contact with normal `n` and tangent `t`: start at `n`, lean forward by the lead angle about `n × t`, sideways by the side tilt about `t`, then fold back onto a `max_tilt` cone about the normal. | High. Short, standard and kernel-free; FluutCAM's exact normals make it better than facet normals. |
| The "stamp" | `multiaxis/stamp.rs`, `retip.rs`, `contour5d.rs`, `parallel5d.rs` | A ball's cut is the same whatever way its shank points, so a 3-axis ball path (waterline, raster) becomes 5-axis by giving each cutting move a tool axis, pivoting about the ball centre. Moves in air and level floors keep the axis upright. | High. Reuses every 3-axis ball strategy FluutCAM already has, with no new contact solver. Only valid for ball tools. |
| Shank avoidance | `multiaxis/avoid.rs` | Where the commanded axis would drive the shank into the part, tilt to the nearest clear axis inside the cone; refuse by name when none clears. | High, as a design. Their test is facet columns; FluutCAM would test against its BVH of exact faces. |
| Turns in air | `multiaxis/stamp_link.rs` | An axis change of more than a set angle between two cutting moves becomes a link: leave along the contact normal, turn in air, come back. | High. A rule FluutCAM's linker (D08) can take directly. |
| Swarf | `multiaxis/swarf.rs`, `ruling.rs` | Finds the ruling direction of a ruled surface from mesh curvature, then cuts with the flank along it. | Medium. FluutCAM knows ruled faces exactly (planes, cylinders, cones, B-spline surfaces of degree 1 in one direction), so it needs none of the curvature fitting. |
| Rotary axis finder | `multiaxis/axis.rs` and four helpers | Fits the axis of a turned part from mesh normals. | Low. FluutCAM reads cylinder and cone axes from the B-rep (rotary setups already do). |
| C unwind | `crates/cl/src/cl/rotary_unwind.rs` | Brings an angle that has wound past its travel back by whole turns, only at a link, written as an orbit the verifier checks. | Medium, for rotary setups with limited A travel. |
| Rotary feed rules | `crates/post/src/post/rotary_cap.rs` | How Fanuc-family controls, TCP mode (G43.4) and LinuxCNC read F on blocks that turn a rotary. | High, as reference for FluutCAM's post engine (D10). |
| Machine kinematics | `crates/post/src/machine/*.rs`, `site/content/guide/multi-axis/machines-and-tcp.md` | A machine file's parts tree: each rotary's axis, travel and the parts under it; head or table is decided by where the spindle hangs. | Medium. FluutCAM's machine definitions (S 02 §2.3) need the same chain for two rotaries. |

**What CamCraft does not do,** from its own guide:
- It solves no inverse kinematics; the post writes the tool axis and the control turns it into angles.
- It has no singularity check.
- It posts simultaneous moves only through controls with tool centre point control (G43.4, Fanuc and Doosan posts); its other posts refuse tilted moves.
- Its geometry is a triangle mesh, so its normals and rulings are estimates.

FluutCAM should match its refusals and do better on geometry. Inverse kinematics and a singularity check are worth adding where CamCraft stops (Task 4).

## Licence

CamCraft is Apache-2.0 only. FluutCAM is MIT OR Apache-2.0. Both licences are permissive, but copying CamCraft code is not free of conditions:

- A copied or adapted file stays under Apache-2.0. It keeps its notice, says it was changed (Apache-2.0 §4(b)), and is listed in `NOTICE` and the third-party licence list.
- Copied files cannot be offered under MIT. Someone taking FluutCAM under MIT would still be bound by Apache-2.0 for those files, so FluutCAM would become partly Apache-only.
- CamCraft's code works on `&[Triangle]` facets and its own path types, so little of it drops into FluutCAM's exact-surface cutter as is.

**Rule for this plan:** reimplement from CamCraft's designs, which carry no licence condition, and name the source in the design docs. Copy code verbatim only when a file is self-contained and worth keeping as Apache-2.0. Each such copy is a separate commit that adds the notice and the `NOTICE` entry, and the owner approves it.

## Global Constraints

All earlier Global Constraints apply. In addition:

- No DeskProto-derived content.
- Simultaneous moves are posted only to controls that declare tool centre point control, or through FluutCAM's own inverse kinematics once Task 4 proves it. Anything else is refused by name, never written with a warning.
- Every new strategy refuses a non-ball tool by name until a flat or bull-nose 5-axis contact model exists.
- Every commit passes `cargo fmt --all --check`, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings`, `cargo test --workspace`, `cargo xtask check-layers`, `cargo deny check` and `cargo xtask wiki-reference --check`.

## Task 1: decisions (owner)

- [ ] **Step 1:** Reopen OQ-16: simultaneous 5-axis moves from "future" to "after 3+2", limited to ball-tool finishing (the stamp) and swarf.
- [ ] **Step 2:** Choose the first target machine and control: a TCP control (Fanuc G43.4, LinuxCNC TCP kinematics) or FluutCAM's own kinematics (Task 4). The choice sets the order of Tasks 4 and 5.
- [ ] **Step 3:** Confirm the licence rule above.

## Task 2: 3+2 with two angles (already in the spec's next release)

- [ ] **Step 1:** An `indexed` setup with A and B (S 03 §3.9.2): the tool axis picked as today (planar face normal, cylinder axis, edge or ±X/±Y/±Z), and the two angles solved for the machine's rotary chain, both solutions offered when there are two.
- [ ] **Step 2:** Machine definitions state the rotary chain: each rotary's axis, travel and whether it carries the table or the head (CamCraft's parts-tree idea). A setup whose angles fall outside travel is refused by name.
- [ ] **Step 3:** Post: index both rotaries between setups (rapid, clamp codes where the machine states them), as the A-only index does today. Golden G-code for a table-table and a head-table machine.
- [ ] **Step 4:** Simulation shows the stock turned by both angles. The verifier checks every setup in its own frame, as it does today.

## Task 3: the tool-axis law and the stamp

- [ ] **Step 1:** `fluut-cam::multiaxis::axis_law`: lead, side tilt and a `max_tilt` cone about the exact surface normal at the contact (D07 gives the contact face and parameter). Property tests: the output is a unit vector inside the cone; zero lead and tilt give the normal; folding never passes the cone.
- [ ] **Step 2:** The stamp: give each cutting move of a ball path a tool axis, pivoting about the ball centre so the cut does not change. Moves in air and level floors stay upright. The result is a `fluut-path` pass with a tool axis per segment (D08 §2 gains an optional axis).
- [ ] **Step 3:** Turns in air: an axis change above a set angle between cutting moves becomes a link along the contact normal (D08 linking).
- [ ] **Step 4:** Strategies: 5-axis parallel, waterline (contour) and scallop, each its 3-axis base plus the stamp. Goldens on the test parts; a check that the ball centres equal the 3-axis path exactly.

## Task 4: kinematics, inverse kinematics and singularities

- [ ] **Step 1:** A kinematics model from the machine definition: the rotary chain, pivot offsets and travel limits; forward kinematics with tests against hand-worked poses.
- [ ] **Step 2:** Inverse kinematics: tool axis to the two rotary angles, both branches; keep the branch along the path that turns least and stays in travel.
- [ ] **Step 3:** Singularities: where the tool axis lines up with the first rotary, the second angle is free and a control can swing it fast. Detect the region, hold the free angle, or refuse by name. CamCraft has no such check, so this is new design work (a D-doc section first).
- [ ] **Step 4:** Without TCP, the post writes the compensating linear moves from the kinematics; with TCP it writes tip positions and axis angles. Feed per CamCraft's rotary feed rules as reference: inverse time (G93) where the control needs it.

## Task 5: shank and holder avoidance

- [ ] **Step 1:** Test the shank and holder of a tilted tool against the part's BVH of exact faces. Where the law's axis collides, search the cone for the nearest clear axis; refuse by name when none clears.
- [ ] **Step 2:** Smooth the chosen axes along the path so they change within a set angle per millimetre; a jump becomes a link (Task 3, step 3).

## Task 6: swarf

- [ ] **Step 1:** Detect ruled faces exactly: planes, cylinders, cones, and B-spline surfaces of degree 1 in one direction. Their rulings are known lines, not estimates.
- [ ] **Step 2:** Flank cutting along the ruling with the tool's side, with a depth offset for the tool's corner; refuse a face whose twist along the cut exceeds what a straight flank can follow within tolerance.

## Task 7: simulation and verification of tilted tools

- [ ] **Step 1:** D11's sweeps assume a fixed tool axis. Add the swept volume of a ball tool whose axis turns along a move (for a ball, the cut is the swept sphere; the shank needs its own sweep for collision only).
- [ ] **Step 2:** The verifier checks gouge, holder and shank collision, and rapids for 5-axis passes; posting refuses a pass the verifier has not passed.

## Task 8: documentation and UAT
